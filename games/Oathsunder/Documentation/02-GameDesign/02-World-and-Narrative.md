## 1. Narrative pillars

| # | Pillar | What it means on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Every oath has a shadow.** | Every vow in the game, kept or broken, has a price someone else pays. We always show who. |
| 2 | **You fight people, not monsters.** | Every boss is a person who kept a promise too long. Each fight ends on their humanity, not on a health bar. |
| 3 | **Mercy costs.** | Restore keeps the living safe and the dead chained. Sunder frees the dead and endangers the living. No option is free, and the game never calls one of them "good". |
| 4 | **The world remembers.** | Oathstone choices, spared rivals and broken promises show up in later regions, in the final boss, in the epilogue and in what companions say to you. |
| 5 | **Two hands, one body.** | Rhen (Ember) and Sable (Umbra) are the game's central relationship. The mechanics, the story and the endings are all about whether the two of them can share one life. |

## 2. World history timeline

Years are counted from the Swearing: **AS** (*After the Swearing*) and **BS** (*Before*). The
present is **500 AS**, the Quincentennial of the Oath. The Solemn Throne has planned the celebrations
for a decade, and it is holding them while the Oath fails around it.

### 2.1 The present crisis (Director's Key)

The Throne believes the Oath is failing because the tithe is too small, so it demands more: the
Living Tithe, the Culling Moots, the Shadow Shifts. **It has the problem backwards.** The keystone
of the seal is Aurem's own severed shadow. It has swallowed every tithed shadow for five hundred
years and grown into **the First Shadow**, Umbra Prime. The Gate is cracking because what it holds
has grown **too full**, not too weak. Every new tithe makes the keystone heavier and the cracks
wider. The Unsworn are shadows squeezed out through those cracks.

This is the Twelvefold Oath's shadow. Each of its twelve lines is a vow of restraint. The thirteenth
clause, written in Umbra between the lines where only Oath-light can show it, reads:

> *"And for this keeping, let every shadow be mine."*

The game's three main endings are three answers to that sentence:

- **Keep it** (Solemn Dawn).
- **Break it** (Unbound Night).
- **Rewrite it** (Rewoven Oath), so that it reads *"let every shadow be lent, and returned."*

The fourth ending (Sable Ascendant) is what happens when the shadow answers instead.

### 2.2 The Twelvefold Oath (full text)

The player assembles these twelve lines as the **Oath Fragments** (§9.1). Each Oathlord has turned
their own line inside out, and each boss fight shows the reversal.

| # | Oathstone | Original line | How its Oathlord inverted it |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Devotion | *What I kneel to, I keep burning.* | Kessh burns what kneels to him. |
| II | Mercy | *What I pull from the water, I do not hold under.* | Ilvane holds children under to "spare" them. |
| III | Labor | *What my hands make, my hands owe nothing.* | Gorran makes workers' hands pay with their shadows. |
| IV | Kinship | *Whom I call blood, I do not bind.* | Ysolde binds her dead sister to herself. |
| V | Faith | *What I cannot see, I will not sell.* | Maal sells pilgrims' shadows to a god he has never seen. |
| VI | Strength | *What I can break, I choose to carry.* | Tharuk breaks the weak so the strong do not have to carry them. |
| VII | Loyalty | *Whom I serve, I may still refuse.* | Veloran seals obedience into masks. |
| VIII | Life | *What lives, I let end.* | Myrrhen lets nothing end. |
| IX | Discipline | *What I hold still, I hold freely.* | Oru holds his students still by tithing their feelings. |
| X | Memory | *What I remember, I return.* | Quill hoards memory and returns none of it. |
| XI | Duty | *What I am ordered, I weigh.* | Hask obeys without weighing. |
| XII | Sovereignty | *What I rule, I do not own.* | Aurem owns every shadow in Varanth. |

## 3. Factions in depth

### 3.1 The Solemn Throne

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seat | The Sun Hall, on top of the Undermourn Gate (`region.solemnthrone`). |
| Head | **Emperor Aurem**, the Solemn Sun, whom the state religion calls "the Sun that does not set". He is openly deathless. What is secret is the reason. |
| Hands | **Chancellor Orsolya Venn** (administration, and author of the Edict of the Living Tithe). **High Tithe-Reader Casimir Dole** (the Tithe Chancery and its Ledger). **Ser Blaise Ondrey** (captain of the Sunward Guard). |
| Structure | Twelve Oathlords rule the regions in the Throne's name. The Chancery taxes coin, and the Tithe Chancery "taxes" shadows. Each region keeps a Tithe Office that feeds the Ashen Choir's rites. |
| Creed | *"The Sun does not set. The Seal does not break. The Tithe is the price of morning."* |
| Iconography | A gold sun-disc with twelve rays, white lacquer, gold leaf. Imperial art never shows shadows, and statues are lit from below so they cast none. |
| View of Rhen | A traitor and a heretic. After he returns, he is also an abomination: a man with two shadows (Sable) where there should be one. |
| Gameplay | Sunward Guard and Tithe-Readers are late-game elites. Warrant posters escalate across regions, and each region's Tithe Office is a mission hub for infiltration objectives. |
| How choices change it | Each **Restore** strengthens imperial control in that region: patrols return, trade reopens, and the tithe continues under a new keeper. Each **Sunder** loosens it: patrols vanish, Tithe Offices burn, and bounties on Rhen rise. |

### 3.2 Oathwardens

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seat | Warden Keep inside Bloodmoon Citadel, plus a chapter house in every region. |
| Head | **Lord-Commander Hask Varrow** (also General of Bloodmoon). **Warden-Captain Idris Hale** leads the field companies. |
| Structure | About 900 sworn knights (there were 4,000 a century ago), in companies of twelve. Each Warden swears a personal vow on their blade. That practice is the origin of **Oathblade Style**. |
| Duty | Guard the Oathstones, escort tithe caravans, and hunt the Unsworn. |
| Creed | *"We are the door's hinges. We do not ask what is on the other side."* |
| Iconography | Black-lacquered armor. A crest showing a sealed door with twelve rivets. Unlit lanterns on the belt, lit only when a Warden dies. |
| View of Rhen | The only Warden in 500 years to refuse a rite. Veterans spit at his name, and recruits whisper it. |
| Gameplay | Rival duels (Idris Hale), elite katana and nodachi enemies, and **Oathwarden Sigils** as collectibles (§9.4). |
| How choices change it | Sundering **Duty** frees every Warden from their vows (§4.11). Many join Rhen's final assault as allies on `mission.solemnthrone.01`. |

### 3.3 The Unsworn

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nature | Shadows forced out of the overfull seal through its cracks. They are cold, hungry for Ember, and drawn to warmth and to their own names. |
| Two kinds | **Husks** are feral and forget everything except hunger. They are the common enemies. **Remnants** remember who they were. They talk, bargain, mourn, and give quests. |
| Notable Remnants | **Old Mother Silt**, a midwife from before the Oath who guides Rhen out of the Undermourn. **Eirmund**, the Pale Stag. **Lady Corvaine**, the Ninth Widow. The **Drowned Choir**. |
| Creed (of the Remnants) | *"We were promised the water. We were given the stone."* |
| Iconography | Ink-black silhouettes. Wet footprints that dry upward. Their outlines drip toward the sky instead of the ground. |
| View of Rhen | Husks see only warmth to devour. Remnants recognize Sable and call him "brother". |
| Gameplay | Husk variants appear in every region (Ash, Drowned, Rivet, Wind, Mirage, Rime, Paper, Glowcap, Gale, Ink, Siege, Gilded). Remnants drive **Shadow Echoes** (§9.3). |
| How choices change it | Each **Sunder** releases a region's tithed dead. Husks become more numerous there, but so do Remnants with names, side quests and reunions. |

### 3.4 The Ashen Choir

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seat | Emberfall Monastery (`region.emberfall`). Chantries in every Tithe Office. |
| Head | **Abbot Kessh**, the Ash Censer. Under him: **Precentor Vashti Coil** (hymn and censer), **Deacon Horvath Pyle** (the headsman) and **Sister Idra** (ash gardens and novices). |
| Structure | Abbot, then Precentors, Deacons, Brothers and Sisters, and Novices. Novices are almost all orphans of tithed parents. |
| Rite | **The Last Rite.** The dying are anointed with ash, the censer is swung over the body, and the smoke draws the shadow out and carries it to the nearest Oathstone. The Choir hums one low note, "the Kneel", for the whole rite. |
| Creed | *"Ash to the stone. Name to the Ledger. Soul to the Seal."* |
| Iconography | Ash-grey robes, faces painted with ash, bronze censers on chains, shaved heads with one lock left uncut "for the tithe". |
| View of Rhen | They executed him. His return is the worst blasphemy they know. |
| Gameplay | Emberfall's enemy roster (Novices, Censer-Bearers, Cinder Penitents). Choir exiles become a Lanternhold side thread after Emberfall. **Brother Adekan**, a defector, hosts Training mode and the Dojo. |
| How choices change it | **Restore Devotion:** Idra reforms the Choir, and the Living Tithe ends inside the Choir. **Sunder Devotion:** the Choir scatters and its exiles turn up as refugees, penitents or raiders in later regions. |

### 3.5 Lantern Guild

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seat | The Guild Exchange, Lanternhold (`region.lanternhold`). |

### 3.6 The Rootbound

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seat | The Heartwood, the Verdant Rot (`region.verdantrot`). |
| Head | **Queen Myrrhen**, the Mycelial Bride, served by **Sporemother Vell** (high priestess) and **Anselk the Groom** (her husband, held upright by the mycelium). |

|---|---|

## 4. The twelve regions

Every region follows the same structure: history, culture, landmarks, arenas, Oathstone, and what
changes on Restore versus Sunder. Arena scene names follow canon asset prefixes (`SC_`). The
**Keeper** is the NPC who swears the stone anew if the player chooses Restore. If the preferred
Keeper is dead because of an earlier choice, the fallback Keeper swears instead.

### 4.1 Emberfall Monastery — `region.emberfall` · Lv 1–5 · Abbot Kessh (`boss.kessh`) · Oathstone of Devotion

**History.** Emberfall was founded in 31 AS, when the first tithe was discovered, and has been the
seat of the Ashen Choir ever since. It is carved into the Ashspine, a dead volcano. Five centuries
of burning tithe-censers have left the mountain permanently grey, and ash falls like snow at every
dusk. Rhen was executed here, and it is also where he climbs back out.

**Culture.** Everything is done by the bell. Monks speak only in hymn inside the cloister. Novices
are orphans of the tithed, raised to perform the rite that took their parents. Pilgrims climb the
Censer Steps carrying their dead on litters, and walk back down carrying ash.

**Landmarks.** The **Censer Steps** (1,200 steps, lined with swinging censers). The **Belltower of
Last Rites** (a great bronze bell whose tone draws shadows into the Oathstone). The **Choir Loft**.
The **Lower Cloister**. The **Ash Gardens**, where tithe-ash is scattered. The **Ossuary Well**,
where the Choir drops the bodies of the tithed and where Rhen climbs out. The **Oathwarden barracks
shrine**.

### 4.2 The Weeping Reeds — `region.weepingreeds` · Lv 5–10 · Mother Ilvane (`boss.ilvane`) · Oathstone of Mercy

**History.** The Reeds are where the Ashwater river, grey with Emberfall's ash, meets the sea across
a hundred miles of salt marsh. In 471 AS the Mourning Flood drowned the chapel villages. Mother
Ilvane, a midwife who lost her three daughters that night, became the marsh's matriarch and founded
the **Houses of Quiet Water**. The Throne thinks they are orphanages. They are where loose-shadowed
children are drowned "gently" before the Choir can take them.

**Culture.** Stilt villages, eel-fishers and reed boats. Funeral barges are poled out into the fog.
Reedfolk sing to the water before they cross it and never say the word "tide". Everyone owns a bell,
and a bell rung three times means someone has gone under.

**Landmarks.** **Hollowmere**, a village that sank and whose roofs still show at low water. The
**Weeping Pier**. The **Bell Causeway**, a rotting mile of planks leading to the chapel. The **House
of Quiet Water**. The **Drowned Chapel**, below the causeway, where three voices still sing.

### 4.3 Ironroot Forge — `region.ironroot` · Lv 10–15 · Gorran Vox (`boss.gorran`) · Oathstone of Labor

**History.** Ironroot is a forge-city built into a live caldera, with molten rivers for streets.
Since the Iron Compact of 377 AS, workers have "paid ahead" on the tithe through **Shadow Shifts**:
their living shadows are clamped to the city's engines, where their fear and fatigue drive the
pistons. In 470 AS Gorran Vox, a smith's apprentice, led the Cinder Rising and broke the guilds,
then put on the crown and doubled the shifts. Below the city sleeps **the Ferrous Maw**, the
largest engine ever built.

**Culture.** Everyone is counted. Workers wear their shift tally stamped on their collarbones, and
the city's song is a hammer rhythm (long, short, short). Ironroot's calendar has no holidays, only
"cold days" when the engines are maintained. The Free Blades' largest pit, **the Slag Pits**, sits
on the edge of the lower district.

**Landmarks.** The **Great Crucible**. The **Cold Forge**, the abandoned forge where Tessen hides.
**Bellows Square**. The **Ledger Hall**. The **Crane Yard**. The **Quenching Vats**. The **Slag
Pits**. A sealed chapel-door stamped with a penitent's hammer, behind which lies the hidden foundry
of Iron Abbess Velka.

### 5.2 Beat outline

**Prologue: The Refusal** (Emberfall 01–03)

1. **The Censer Steps.** Oathwarden Rhen escorts eight-year-old Liss up the mountain with Warden-Captain
   Idris Hale's company. She asks whether it will hurt. He tells her the truth: he doesn't know.
2. **The order.** At the Belltower, Abbot Kessh reads the Edict of the Living Tithe. Liss's loose
   shadow is to be cut from her while she lives. Rhen refuses ("Not while she's breathing") and fights
   his own company. This is the tutorial. Idris disarms him in a scripted loss.
3. **Seven Steps to the Block.** Bound at the wrists, Rhen fights his way down the steps to the block
   using kicks, grabs and guard. Horvath Pyle reads Hask Varrow's writ. Kessh swings the censer over
   him, and the blade falls.
4. **The first refusal.** The censer smoke reaches for Rhen's shadow and **the shadow tears itself
   away** and falls into the Ossuary Well with his body. Liss screams. Brother Adekan's hands shake
   around the rite-knife he was meant to use on her.
5. **The Drowned Stair.** Rhen wakes in black water. A figure with his face and a crooked grin stands
   over him. It is Sable. Old Mother Silt, a Remnant midwife, tells him: "The door's cracked, love.
   You're not the first to climb. You're the first to climb *whole*." They climb together. On the way
   up Rhen learns Umbral Shadow and Perfect Dodge, and in the stair pool he lands his first Execution
   on the Stair-Eel. It has been forty days.

**Act I: Ash and Water**

- *Emberfall 04–10.* Rhen holds the Ossuary Well until the dawn bell and learns Ember Rage. He finds
  Adekan, who smuggled Liss downriver to Mother Ilvane's House of Quiet Water, believing it a refuge.
  Rhen duels Precentor Vashti Coil, who is defeated and flees, and then Deacon Horvath, the man who
  executed him. He climbs the burning Censer Steps, where Idris lets him pass for one breath of old
  brotherhood. He kills Kessh at the Belltower. **First Oathstone choice.**
- *Weeping Reeds 01–10.* Rhen chases a Choir tithe-barge downriver and meets Neve on a smuggling
  skiff. He learns what the House of Quiet Water really is, rescues Liss from the drowning pools, and
  meets **Moth**, Liss's loose shadow. **Tamsin of the Nine Knives** attacks for the first time and
  pins Sable with a knife, and Sable feels pain. The Drowned Chapel sings with three voices. Ilvane's
  mercy is a mother's grief turned into a system. **Second Oathstone choice.**

**Act II: Iron, Silk and Glass**

- *Ironroot.* Tessen joins at the Cold Forge and Oskar Dray at the Slag Pits. The Shadow Shifts show
  that the tithe is being taken from the living in advance. Tamsin steals the forge-seal key in a
  crane-line chase. Gorran falls, and the Ferrous Maw stirs beneath the Crucible.
- *Silkwind.* Mireth Vale joins in the Loom Library. The Binding Rite stitches the dead onto the
  living. Tamsin hunts Rhen through the night bamboo. The Paired Petals turn out to be one living
  woman and one refusal.
- *Glass Ossuary.* Faith sold by the ounce. Rhen and Tamsin are stranded in a glass-storm and fight
  side by side; they part at dawn with something like respect. The hidden tomb of Ossric is found.
  **Act turn:** under the Sleepers' bones, Liss hears the Rebirth Song and understands that the dead
  once came back.

**Act III: Wolves, Masks and Roots**

- *Rimewood.* Rhen's old mentor Ser Mathilde Crowe tests him. The Culling Moots are exposed, and a
  pale stag leads Rhen to a hidden grove. Tamsin is dying (she sold half her shadow) and tells him
  who hired her: Duke Veloran, acting for the Throne. Kirra Ashfang, Tharuk's daughter, can be
  spared. The Winter Wolf falls.
- *Lanternhold.* Neve comes home to a city of masks. The shadow market trades in collateral cut from
  living shadows. **Tamsin's final duel on the rooftops**: spare her or kill her. The Duke's mask comes
  off, and there is no one under it. The Masque Oath binds loyalty with shadow-slivers.
- *Verdant Rot.* A wedding that is a funeral. Liss meets Tuck, a moss-child, and learns how to say
  goodbye. Isketh's hive lies beneath. Myrrhen was sold to the Rot as a girl and has been kept alive
  ever since. **Act turn:** the Rootbound prove shadows *can* be kept from the tithe, but only by
  denying them rest. Neither the Throne's answer nor the Rot's is the right one.

**Act IV: Wind and Memory**

- *Cloudspire.* A school that tithes feelings. Imre Tal can be spared. Oru takes his first step in
  forty years and dies smiling. His last words send Rhen to the summit and Sen Ajari.
- *Sunken Archive.* Mireth faces her old master. Lotte Brenn flees with the Ledger of the Tithed,
  and Hanne's name is missing from it. **Act turn:** in `mission.sunkenarchive.09` Quill shows the
  thirteenth clause. The keystone is Aurem's own shadow, the Gate cracks because it is too *full*,
  and every tithe makes it worse. Sable understands what will happen to him if Rhen ever takes the
  Throne.

**Act V: Blood and Sun**

- *Bloodmoon Citadel.* The capital is under siege. Idris Hale fights Rhen twice; the second time,
  Rhen chooses whether he lives. Sable asks for **Terms**. Hask Varrow, the man who taught Rhen to
  hold a sword and then signed his death, fights him under the red moon and asks Rhen to guide the
  blade.
- *Solemn Throne.* The Chancellor's betrayal. The Tithe Chancery. Liss's shadow is drawn toward the
  Gate. **Twelve Echoes**: the Sovereignty stone calls up every Oathlord Rhen has killed. Aurem
  fights with the power of every stone Rhen restored, and every stone he sundered fights beside Rhen.
  **The last Oathstone choice.**

## 6. The Restore / Sunder moral choice system

### 6.1 The Oath Choice moment

1. **Execution.** The Oathlord's fight ends in their exclusive execution (see `05-Bosses.md`). A
   shattered Oathstone cannot be used, so the finisher never damages the stone.
2. **The Hearing.** A short cinematic in front of the Oathstone. The stone projects its line of the
   Oath in Oath-light. Two or three companions give their view: each companion speaks at most once,
   and whoever has the strongest stake in the region speaks last. Sable always speaks, in a whisper.
3. **The Keeper.** The Restore option names the Keeper who will swear the stone (§4). The player
   hears the Keeper's new vow *before* choosing.
4. **The Choice.** Two options only, **Restore** and **Sunder**, shown in neutral colors (bone-white
   and ink-black, never gold versus red). There is no timer. The player confirms by holding the
   button for 2 s. **The choice is permanent for that save.**
5. **The Ledger.** The Oath Ledger UI animates the change: a stone lights or cracks, and the Balance
   dial moves one notch. Companion approval changes are shown as small sigils, never as numbers.
6. **The Aftermath.** The region changes state (§4), and the next time the hub loads the new state
   is visible: weather, NPCs, merchants, Unsworn density, Remnant quests.

### 6.2 Oath Balance

```
restoreCount  = number of Oathstones restored   (0..12)
sunderCount   = number of Oathstones sundered   (0..12)
oath.balance  = restoreCount - sunderCount      (-12..+12; each choice moves it by exactly 1)
```

| Band (mid-campaign) | Balance | Visual on Rhen | Effect | World reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sun-leaning** | +3 … +12 | Scar-veins glow gold, and Ember sparks trail his blade | Ember meter gain +10% | Imperial NPCs use honorifics, merchants give discounts, Remnants flee. |
| **Dusk** | −2 … +2 | Veins shift between gold and ink with his stance | Ember and Umbra gain +5%, and the **Twilight rune socket** is active (07-Progression §10) | Mixed. Both sides test him. |
| **Night-leaning** | −3 … −12 | Veins run ink-black, and Sable's silhouette looks darker | Umbra meter gain +10% | Remnants approach and speak. Imperial patrols attack on sight. |

**Equal-budget rule.** Every band, and every Restore or Sunder reward (the rune lines in §4), is sized
to the same power budget. They differ in flavor and playstyle, never in strength. The Dusk band spends
its budget as a smaller meter bonus plus the Twilight socket, and no combat mechanic is locked behind
Restore or Sunder.

At campaign end (all 12 chosen) the balance is always even. The ending thresholds in §8 are defined
on the counts, and the balance equivalents are given for UI and telemetry.

## 7. Branching dialogue system

### 7.1 Goals

- Choices change **people** (approval, flags, who lives), and only Oath nodes change **the world
  order** (the Balance).
- **Silence is always a valid answer** in any scene that matters.
- Dialogue feeds combat. Pre-fight stances and mid-fight lines change boss behavior, and the effects
  are readable on screen.
- No morality colors and no "Paragon/Renegade" meters. Consequences are shown in the fiction first.

### 7.2 Node types

| Node | Purpose | Key fields | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `line` | A spoken line | `speaker` (`char.*` ID), `vo` (e.g. `VO_Sable_Reeds_Barge_04`), `emotion`, `camera` | Lines longer than 12 s are split. |
| `choice` | 2–4 player responses | `options[]`, optional `timer` (4–8 s), `silence` target | A timed choice resolves to its `silence` branch when time runs out. |
| `branch` | Invisible router | ordered `conditions → target` list, `default` | Evaluated top to bottom. |
| `whisper` | Sable interjection | `line`, optional `heed` / `ignore` responses | At most one per scene. Heed/ignore gives ±2 Sable approval. Can overlay gameplay. |
| `challenge` | Pre-fight stance | `salute` · `taunt` · `silence` · `plead` (plead only where authored) | Applies the boss's authored stance modifier for Phase I (§7.5). |
| `clash` | Mid-combat exchange | trigger (`phase`, `hp%`, `event`), `line`, optional one-button `reply` | Non-blocking, and never pauses the simulation. |
| `mercy` | Spare or kill | `target` (`char.*`), `kill` (hold `Execute`), `spare` (hold `Guard`) | Sets `flag.<char>.spared`. The camera holds on the target's face. |
| `oath` | Restore or Sunder | `oathstone`, `keeper`, `keeperVow` line | **The only node that changes Oath Balance.** 2 s hold to confirm. |
| `lore` | Knowledge-gated option | `requires` (scroll, fragment, echo), `option` | Shown with a seal icon. Never a better outcome, only a truer one. |
| `echo` | Plays a Shadow Echo | `echo` ID | Used in cinematics and companion scenes. |
| `set` | Consequences | `flags`, `approval`, `items`, `unlocks`, `aiMods`, `merchantMods` | Invisible. |
| `end` | Exit | `returnTo` (gameplay, cinematic, mission) | |

### 7.3 Conditions

A condition is a boolean expression over save state:

```
flag.tamsin.spared == true
approval.neve >= 40
oath.balance <= -3
fragments.count == 12
flag.oathstone.mercy == sundered
mission.complete(mission.silkwind.09)
mastery(weapon.naginata) >= 3
has(echo.weepingreeds.03)
```

Operators: `== != < <= > >=`, `&&`, `||`, `!`, and parentheses. Unknown flags evaluate as `unclaimed`
or `false` and never as errors. Authors test every branch against the four **reference saves**:
Sun (12 restores), Night (12 sunders), Dusk (6/6 with everything collected) and Bare (6/6 with
nothing collected).

### 7.4 Consequences

| Consequence | Example | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Flag set | `flag.kirra.spared = true` | Flags are write-once unless marked `mutable`. |
| Approval | `approval.oskar += 10` | Clamped to −100…+100. |
| Item | `give(knife.ix)` | |
| Unlock | `unlock(side.lanternhold.05)` | |
| AI modifier | `aiMod(boss.hask, patience, -15, phase=1)` | Phase-scoped. |
| Merchant | `merchant(char.obed, price, -20%)` | |
| Epilogue variable | `epilogue(char.tamsin, "sold-back-shadow")` | |
| Oath | `oath(oathstone.duty, restored, keeper=char.idris)` | `oath` node only. |

### 7.5 Pre-fight stances (the `challenge` node)

| Stance | Default effect on the boss during Phase I | Typical line |
|---|---|---|
| **Salute** | Showmanship +10, Aggression −5. The boss opens with a readable signature move. | "Oathwarden Rhen, formerly. Ready when you are." |
| **Taunt** | Aggression +15, Patience −15. More openings, and harder hits. | "Your god's never going to look at you." |
| **Silence** | No change. | *(Rhen draws.)* |
| **Plead** | Authored only for Hask, Tamsin, Idris and the Paired Petals. A one-time hesitation (the boss skips its first attack) and a unique `clash` line. | "You don't have to do this." |

Every boss in `05-Bosses.md` can override these defaults.

### 7.7 Authoring rules

1. VO IDs follow canon: `VO_<Speaker>_<Scene>_<nn>`.
2. Every scene with a `choice` also authors a `silence` branch, even if it only leads to a shorter
   version of the same outcome.
3. Sable whispers **never** reveal information the player has not been able to find, and never
   point at the "right" option.
4. No companion approval change larger than ±20 from a single node.
5. A `mercy` node is never timed.
6. Every Oathlord gets at least one `clash` exchange per phase.

## 8. Endings

### 8.1 Conditions

| Ending ID | Name | Canon condition | Formal check |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ending.solemndawn` | **The Solemn Dawn** | Restore ≥ 9 Oathstones, or a balanced Rhen swears the Final Vow of Restoration at the Sealed Gate. Rhen takes the Throne; the cycle continues. | (`oath.restorecount >= 9` (Balance ≥ +6) **or** `flag.vow.restoration`), and `finale.gate` won. |
| `ending.unboundnight` | **The Unbound Night** | Sunder ≥ 9 Oathstones, or a balanced Rhen swears the Final Vow of Sundering at the Sealed Gate. All oaths break; the dead walk free. | (`oath.sundercount >= 9` (Balance ≤ −6) **or** `flag.vow.sundering`), and `finale.gate` won. |
| `ending.rewovenoath` | **The Rewoven Oath** (true) | Balanced choices + Liss's questline + all 12 Oath Fragments → defeat The First Shadow | Balanced = `4 <= oath.restorecount <= 8` (Balance −4…+4), **and** `flag.quest.liss.complete`, **and** `fragments.count == 12`, **and** `finale.gate` won, **and** `boss.firstshadow` defeated in `finale.rewovenoath`. |
| `ending.sableascendant` | **Sable Ascendant** (secret) | Yield to Sable in the mirror duel. | `finale.gate` result is `yield` (the player took Sable's hand during the Offer). Checked first, and it overrides every other condition. |

"Balanced" is defined as exactly the band neither canon threshold reaches, so **every possible
combination of the 12 choices maps to exactly one canon ending route**, and the Final Vow (§8.3)
guarantees that a balanced player can always finish the story.

### 8.2 Flow

```
mission.solemnthrone.10  (Aurem)  ->  Oath node: Sovereignty  ->  route is fixed
  restorecount >= 9  -> Dawn route
  sundercount  >= 9  -> Night route
  otherwise          -> Balanced route -> Sealed Gate (8.3): Rewoven requirements met, or Final Vow
every route -> finale.gate (mirror duel, boss.sableascendant)
  yield -> finale.sableascendant       -> ending.sableascendant
  win   -> Dawn:     finale.solemndawn   -> ending.solemndawn
           Night:    finale.unboundnight -> ending.unboundnight
           Balanced: finale.rewovenoath  -> defeat boss.firstshadow -> ending.rewovenoath
```

### 8.3 The Sealed Gate rule (Balanced route)

A Rhen who refused both thrones has only one road left, and it goes down. On the Balanced route the
Gate opens to the Rewoven path only when `flag.quest.liss.complete` and `fragments.count == 12` are
both true. Until then the **Oathless Camp** in the Gate Undercroft is a hub with fast travel to every
region. The Gate's face shows twelve empty fragment sockets and Liss's ribbon, and each one lights as
it is completed.

**The Final Vow.** At any time before the Rewoven requirements are met, Rhen can kneel at the Gate and
swear a Final Vow — *of Restoration* (`flag.vow.restoration`) or *of Sundering* (`flag.vow.sundering`).
The vow is presented as a deliberate, voiced, twice-confirmed choice with Sable and Liss reacting, and
it commits the save to the Dawn or Night route (`finale.gate` then `finale.solemndawn` /
`finale.unboundnight`). Liss's farewell scene plays in both vows, so her questline still pays off.
Players who want the true ending keep exploring; players who don't are never held hostage by
collectibles. The Rewoven path stays fair through three design guarantees:

- **Every Oath Fragment is permanent and reachable.** None is missable, none sits behind a hidden
  boss or a raid, and regions can be revisited.
- **Liss's questline cannot fail.** Every step can be delayed, none can be lost, and approval never
  locks it.
- **The Codex Lens** (reward of `mission.sunkenarchive.10`) marks every unfound fragment on the map.
  Before that, Liss's *Fragment Sense* (from `quest.liss.03`) makes her hum louder within 30 m of
  one.

### 8.4 After the credits

The save returns to the Oathless Camp before `finale.gate`, and the ending is recorded in the Oath
Ledger. Oath choices stay locked on that save, but the player can:

- replay `finale.gate`, which is how most players will find Sable Ascendant;
- finish collectibles, hidden duels and raids (`raid.hundredhanded` opens after any ending).

**New Game+ "The Second Oath"** resets choices and story flags, and keeps weapon mastery,
cosmetics and the Echo Codex.

### 9.3 Shadow Echoes (72)

A **Shadow Echo** (collectible, `echo.<regionshort>.<nn>`, six per region) is a dead shadow
replaying its last moment. It is not the canon combat effect "shadow echo" (Sable's delayed strike
in Umbral Shadow), though in the fiction they are the same thing: *a shadow repeating what it once
did.*

- **Finding them.** Echo sites are ripples of black water on a floor or wall, visible only when
  Sable is attuned. Out of combat, within 10 m of a site, he says something like "Someone's still
  talking."
- **Listening.** Out of combat, the player holds `Shadow` near the site. Rhen kneels and Sable's
  silhouette re-enacts the moment while the audio plays (15–60 s, subtitled). The echo goes into the
  **Echo Codex** and can be replayed from camp.
- **Chains.** Some echoes unlock side missions. The three **Hanne echoes** (`echo.weepingreeds.01`,
  `echo.glassossuary.04`, `echo.sunkenarchive.06`) drive Liss's questline.

| 2 | Who is The First Shadow? | Aurem's severed shadow, the keystone of the seal, grown into Umbra Prime by 500 years of tithe. | It is thematically the Oath's own shadow (the tagline), and a dark mirror of Rhen and Sable. |
